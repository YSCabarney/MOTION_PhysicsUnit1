SECTION 1 — MOTION GRAPHS

Reading Motion Graphs

A motion graph is a snapshot of how something moves — captured in a picture. The trick is knowing which two quantities are being compared on each axis, and what the shape of the line tells you.

The one rule that covers everything

Slope = rate of change. On a position-time graph, slope equals speed. On a speed-time graph, slope equals acceleration. Get comfortable with slope and you can read any motion graph.

Scenario: a striker's run toward goal

A striker starts from rest at midfield. They wait 2 seconds, then sprint hard for 4 seconds, then slow to a stop in 2 more seconds. Below, see how that same motion looks on three different graphs side by side.

Same motion — three different graph types

Phase 1: standing still (0–2 s)  |  Phase 2: sprinting (2–6 s)  |  Phase 3: slowing down (6–8 s)

POSITION vs. TIME

Flat → steep → less steep. Slope = speed. Steeper = faster.

SPEED vs. TIME

Zero → rising → falling back to zero. Height of line = speed at that moment.

ACCELERATION vs. TIME

Zero → positive burst → negative (braking) → zero. Acceleration is the slope of the speed-time graph.

Scenario: a perfectly weighted pass

A ball rolls at constant speed across the pitch — no speeding up, no slowing down. Here's what that looks like on all three graph types:

Constant speed — three graphs

The ball moves the same distance every second from start to finish.

POSITION vs. TIME

Perfectly straight diagonal line. Constant slope = constant speed.

SPEED vs. TIME

Flat horizontal line. Speed doesn't change. Slope = 0 = no acceleration.

ACCELERATION vs. TIME

Flat line on the zero axis. No acceleration at all.

Scenario: goalkeeper holding the ball

The keeper stands still, ball in hand. Absolutely no motion. Three graphs, all telling the same story:

Stationary object — three graphs

POSITION vs. TIME

Flat horizontal line. Position never changes. Slope = 0.

SPEED vs. TIME

Flat on the zero axis. No speed at all.

ACCELERATION vs. TIME

Flat on the zero axis. Not accelerating.

4-step method for any graph question

Name the axes. What's on y? What's on x? This determines what slope means.

Describe the shape. Going up? Flat? Curved? Multiple segments?

Calculate the slope if needed. Pick two clear points. Slope = rise ÷ run = Δy ÷ Δx.

Translate. Position-time slope → speed. Speed-time slope → acceleration. State units.

Practice Problems — Motion Graphs

Problem 1 — Position-Time (rolling ball)

A ball rolls across the floor. At t = 0 s it is at 0 m. At t = 2 s it is at 2 m. At t = 5 s it is at 5 m. The graph is a straight line.

A. Which phrase best describes speed? (increasing / constant / not moving)
B. How far did the ball roll by t = 4 s?
C. What is the speed? Show slope calculation.

▶ Show Answer

A. Speed is constant — straight line = constant slope = constant speed.

B. 4 m (1 m per second pattern).

C. Slope = (5 − 0) ÷ (5 − 0) = 1.0 m/s

Problem 2 — Speed-Time (car accelerating)

A car starts from rest. At 1 h → 10 mph; at 2 h → 20 mph; at 4 h → 40 mph. Straight line graph.

A. Which phrase best describes the motion?
B. How far did the car travel in 4 hours? (Hint: area = ½ × base × height)
C. How fast at exactly t = 4 h?

▶ Show Answer

A. Speed is increasing — upward slope = positive acceleration.

B. Area = ½ × 4 × 40 = 80 miles

C. Read the graph at t = 4 h: 40 mph

Problem 3 — Soccer Scenario ⚽

A midfielder stands still 3 s, sprints 30 m in 5 s, then decelerates to a stop over 2 s. Describe the slope of each segment on a position-time graph and what each means.

▶ Show Answer

0–3 s: Flat. Slope = 0. Not moving.

3–8 s: Steep diagonal. Slope = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 m/s. Sprinting.

8–10 s: Less steep, still rising. Speed decreasing → slope decreasing. Slowing to stop.

SECTION 2 — FREE FALL & GRAVITY

Free Fall & Gravity

Every ball you kick enters free fall the moment it leaves your foot. Every header, every goal kick, every corner — gravity is always pulling, always accelerating that ball downward at the same relentless rate.

What is gravity?

Gravity is a force of attraction between any two objects that have mass. Earth has enormous mass, which is why it pulls everything toward its center. It depends on two things:

  • Mass: more mass → stronger pull. The Sun keeps Earth in orbit because of its colossal mass.
  • Distance: farther apart → weaker pull. Double the distance → gravity weakens by a factor of 4 (inverse square law).

⚽ Soccer Example

A 75 kg striker weighs about 735 N on Earth. On the Moon (g = −1.6 m/s²) they'd weigh only 120 N. Their mass stays 75 kg everywhere — but they could jump six times higher on the Moon. Headers would be spectacular.

Galileo's discovery — mass doesn't affect fall rate

Galileo's Law of Free Fall

All objects fall at the same rate regardless of mass — when gravity is the only force acting. A 5 kg medicine ball and a tennis ball dropped from the same height hit the ground at exactly the same time.

Building up to the math — step by step

Understand free fall. Only gravity acts — no air resistance, no spin, no other forces. The only acceleration is g = −9.8 m/s² (downward).

Acceleration is constant, speed is not. g never changes. But the object's speed keeps increasing — 9.8 m/s added every single second.

Speed and distance both grow — but differently. Speed grows linearly with time (v = gt). Distance grows with time squared (d = ½gt²). This means falling gets much faster very quickly.

Know what you're given. Every problem gives you some variables. Identify them first, then pick the formula that uses those to find what you're missing.

Signs matter. In this course, down = negative. g = −9.8 m/s². A ball thrown up has positive velocity but negative acceleration (gravity always pulls down).

The formulas — broken down completely

FORMULA 1 — Speed after t seconds of free fall

v = g × t
v = final speed  [meters per second, m/s] — how fast at that exact moment
g = −9.8 m/s² — acceleration due to gravity, constant on Earth (down = negative)
t = time falling  [seconds, s]

Why? Acceleration = speed gained per second. After t seconds you've gained g m/s each second → total speed = g × t.
⚽ Example

A goalkeeper punts the ball and it falls for 2 seconds: v = (−9.8)(2) = −19.6 m/s (downward, ~44 mph) when it reaches a striker's head.

FORMULA 2 — Distance fallen from rest

d = ½ × g × t²
d = distance fallen  [meters, m] — total drop from starting point
g = −9.8 m/s²
t² = time squared — because the object accelerates, each second covers more ground than the last

Why t²? Distance under constant acceleration grows with the square of time — not linearly. At t=1: 4.9 m. At t=2: 19.6 m (4× more, not 2×). This is the signature of acceleration.
⚽ Example

Ball knocked off a 44.1 m stadium wall. After 2 s: d = ½ × (−9.8) × 4 = −19.6 m (19.6 m downward) — already past halfway down.

FORMULA 3 — When you know distance but not time

v² = 2 × g × d
Rearrange to: v = √(2gd)
Use when: you know how far something fell and need the speed at impact — no time required.

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation — inverse square

Newton showed gravity follows an inverse square law: double the distance, and gravity becomes 4 times weaker.

Change in distanceNew gravity strength
Double (×2)1/4 as strong
Triple (×3)1/9 as strong
Half (÷2)4× as strong
Quarter (÷4)16× as strong
Sign Convention

In this course: down = negative. So g = −9.8 m/s². When a ball is thrown upward, velocity is positive (+) but acceleration is −9.8 m/s² the whole time. At the peak, velocity = 0 but acceleration is still −9.8 m/s² — gravity never stops.

Practice Problems — Free Fall

Problem 4 ⚽ — Stadium drop

A soccer ball falls off a 44.1 m stadium wall. Using g = −9.8 m/s²:
A. How long to hit the ground? (d = ½gt², solve for t)
B. How fast at impact? (v = gt)

▶ Show Answer

A. −44.1 = ½ × (−9.8) × t² → −44.1 = −4.9t² → t² = 9 → t = 3 s

B. v = (−9.8)(3) = −29.4 m/s (downward, ≈ 66 mph)

Problem 5 — Speed to distance

A ball is caught when it's moving at 19.6 m/s. How far did it fall from rest?

▶ Show Answer

v² = 2gd → (−19.6)² = 2 × (−9.8) × d → 384.16 = −19.6d → d = −19.6 m (19.6 m downward)

Problem 6 — Complete the table

Fill in the free fall table for t = 2, 3, and 4 seconds using v = gt and d = ½gt².

▶ Show Answer

t = 2: v = 19.6 m/s  |  d = 19.6 m

t = 3: v = 29.4 m/s  |  d = 44.1 m

t = 4: v = 39.2 m/s  |  d = 78.4 m
