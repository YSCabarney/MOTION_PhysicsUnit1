Wile E. Coyote Runs Off a Cliff 🏔️
Looney Tunes  ·  Concepts: free fall · gravity · Newton's 1st Law

When Wile E. runs off the cliff, he keeps moving forward in mid-air for a moment before he falls. Which statement best describes what should actually happen, and why?
Q2Multiple Choice · Free Fall Calculation2 pts
In the cartoon, Wile E. appears to fall for about 4 seconds before hitting the ground. Using d = ½gt², and neglecting air resistance, how tall would that cliff actually be?
Q3Free Body Diagram + Written3 pts
Draw a free body diagram (on paper) for Wile E. at the exact moment his feet leave the cliff edge — mid-air, before he has fallen at all. Then describe it below.
Which forces are present? Which forces are NOT present, and why? What does the net force tell you about his acceleration at that moment?
↑ Hint: what is touching him? What is NOT touching him?
Fg (weight)
FN (normal)
Fa (applied)
Fair (air resistance)
Recorded. Correct answer: only Fg (weight) pointing straight down. No FN — there is no surface in contact with him. No Fa — running ended when he left the cliff. Minimal Fair at the first instant. Net force = Fg downward → acceleration = g = 9.8 m/s² downward, immediately.

The Matrix — Lobby Fight Scene 🥋
The Matrix (1999)  ·  Concepts: Newton's 2nd & 3rd Law · F = ma · action-reaction pairs
▶ Watch: The Matrix — Lobby Scene (Movieclips, ~3 min) Official Movieclips channel · Pay close attention to what happens to Neo vs. what happens to the guards he hits. Watch how they react differently.
Q6Multiple Choice · Newton's 3rd Law2 pts
Neo kicks a guard and the guard flies backward across the room. Neo barely moves. According to Newton's Third Law, what is wrong with this picture?
Q7Calculation + Analysis — Show Your Work3 pts
Suppose Neo (mass: 73 kg) kicks a guard (mass: 91 kg) with a force of 1,100 N.
By Newton's 3rd Law, what force does the guard exert back on Neo? Then use F = ma to find the acceleration of each person. Finally, explain: does the movie show this correctly? What does it get wrong?
Recorded. Force on guard = 1,100 N forward. Force on Neo = 1,100 N backward (same magnitude, opposite direction — 3rd Law). Guard: a = 1100/91 ≈ 12.1 m/s². Neo: a = 1100/73 ≈ 15.1 m/s². Neo has less mass so he should accelerate MORE backward — the opposite of what the movie shows.

Apollo 13 (1995)  ·  Concepts: Newton's 1st Law · inertia · free body diagrams · weight vs. mass

Q8Multiple Choice · Weight vs. Mass · Newton's 1st Law2 pts
In the film, the astronauts and objects appear "weightless" and float inside the capsule. Which of the following best explains what is physically happening? (This one is actually a mix of right and wrong in the movie — read carefully.)
Q9Free Body Diagram + Written2 pts
Draw two free body diagrams on paper — then describe them below.
Situation A: An astronaut standing on Earth before launch (at rest on the launch pad).
Situation B: The same astronaut floating inside the capsule in orbit.
Situation A: On the launch pad
Situation B: In orbit
Recorded. Situation A: Fg (down) + FN (up), equal length arrows → ΣF = 0, at rest. Situation B: Fg (down) only — gravity still acts! But no FN because no surface contact → ΣF = Fg downward → constant free fall. Mass is the same in both; weight (Fg = mg) is similar but slightly less in orbit. The astronaut is not "weightless" — they are in perpetual free fall.

Star Wars — Debris After an Explosion in Space 💥
Star Wars (various)  ·  Concepts: Newton's 1st Law · inertia · sound in vacuum
▶ Watch: Star Wars space battle — Death Star explosion scene (Star Wars official, ~1 min) Star Wars official channel · Watch the explosion and what happens to the debris afterward. Also notice the sounds.
Q10Multiple Choice · Newton's 1st Law in Space2 pts
After an explosion in space, we see debris pieces slow down and stop floating after a few seconds. We also hear a loud boom. Identify TWO things that are physically wrong with this scene.
Q11Short Answer — Written2 pts
A piece of debris from the Death Star explosion has a mass of 500 kg. After the blast, it is moving at 120 m/s to the right, in deep space with no other forces acting on it.
What is its velocity 10 minutes later? What is its acceleration? Draw a free body diagram for this piece of debris on paper and describe it below. Use Newton's Laws to explain your answer — not just common sense.
Recorded. Velocity after 10 minutes: still 120 m/s to the right — unchanged. Acceleration: 0 m/s² (no change in velocity). FBD: no forces (or only negligible gravity from distant objects) → ΣF = 0. Newton's 1st Law: an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity unless acted on by an unbalanced force. With no air resistance or other force in deep space, nothing changes its motion.
