Question guide

  • Q4: ΣF = m·a has the form y = (slope)·x, so the slope is m. The intercept should be about 0.
  • Q5: At 20 N every crate stays put (A barely). Static friction adjusts to match the push, so ΣF = 0.
  • Q6: Only the net force accelerates the crate. Dividing Fa by a gives a mass that's too big. Graphing Fa instead gives a y-intercept equal to the friction force.
  • Q7: Timer rounding (±0.01 s matters most at high forces and short times), reaction time in a real lab, and friction not being perfectly constant.
Common mistake to watch for: graphing Fa instead of Fnet. The slope still comes out near m, but the y-intercept equals the friction force. That's a great Zoom discussion point!

  • Q9: Constant velocity → a = 0 → FN = mg (Newton's 1st law). The scale only responds to acceleration, not speed.
  • Q10: 8–10 s, slowing while going up (a points down): 429 N < 539 N.
  • Q11: Free fall: FN = m(9.8 − 9.8) = 0 N. The rider feels weightless.

Zoom discussion prompts

  1. Put everyone's crate masses in the chat. Did the same-letter crates agree? Why not exactly?
  2. "The elevator is moving fastest during 4–8 s. Why isn't the scale reading highest then?"
  3. Show the sandbox: push 60 N right with 15 N friction on a 10 kg crate. What's the acceleration? Now add a 20 N push to the left.
  4. Bridge to next week: "What if the rope pulled at 30° above horizontal? Which part of the pull lifts the crate, and which part moves it?"
