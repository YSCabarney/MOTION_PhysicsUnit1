Before You Start

Quick Refresher

You already know the difference between a scalar (just a size) and a vector (a size and a direction). Keep that distinction close — acceleration is a vector too, and its direction is what makes it trickier than it first sounds.

Speed scalar

How fast something moves. 25 m/s — no direction attached.

Velocity vector

Speed with a direction. 25 m/s east.

Acceleration vector

The rate velocity changes — in size, direction, or both.

Time interval scalar

How long the change takes, symbol t, in seconds.

Concept One

What Is Acceleration?

In everyday speech "acceleration" just means speeding up. In physics it means something more precise: any change in velocity over time — speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction all count.

Acceleration
a = (vf − vo) / t
a = acceleration  ·  vf = final velocity  ·  vo = original velocity  ·  t = time interval

Where the unit comes from

m/s÷s=m/s²

A change in velocity (m/s) divided by a time (s) leaves meters per second, per second — every second, the velocity itself changes by that many m/s.

Accelerated vs. non-accelerated motion

A ball rolling at a constant velocity covers equal distances in equal times — its positions, sketched once per second, land evenly spaced. An accelerating ball's positions spread farther apart each second (speeding up) or crowd closer together (slowing down).

Position every 1 second
Check: the constant-velocity ball

A ball moving at constant velocity travels 1 meter every second. At the 5‑meter mark:

speed = 1 m/s  ·  time = 5 seconds

Because velocity never changes, the sketch is evenly spaced — this is non-accelerated motion, even though the ball is moving.

Concept One, continued

Three Ways to Accelerate

Because acceleration is a vector, any of these three changes counts — and the sign of a tells you which one is happening.

🚗💨

Speeding Up

Velocity and acceleration point the same direction. a is positive (in the direction of motion).

Merging onto a highway
🛑

Slowing Down

Acceleration points opposite to velocity — often called deceleration.

A car braking at a red light
🎢

Changing Direction

Speed can stay constant while velocity — and therefore acceleration — still changes.

A car rounding a curve at steady speed
i

Real world: free fall is acceleration too. Any object in free fall near Earth's surface gains about 9.8 m/s of speed every second — that constant is itself an acceleration, g = 9.8 m/s².

Applying the Idea

Worked Example: Sprinting Speeds

Speed problems are acceleration's simpler cousin — no change in velocity, just distance ÷ time. The table below has one blank per row; each blank needs a different rearrangement of the same relationship, which is exactly the skill the next section builds.

AnimalDistanceTimeSpeed
Cheetah75 m3.0 s25 m/s
Greyhound160 m10. s16 m/s
Gazelle1.0 km36 s100. km/h
Turtle0.30 m30. s1.0 cm/s
Show how each blank was solved

Greyhound — speed is missing, so use speed = distance/time directly: 160 m ÷ 10. s = 16 m/s.

Gazelle — time is missing, so the formula had to be rearranged: speed = distance/time → time = distance/speed. 1.0 km ÷ 100. km/h = 0.01 h = 36 s.

Turtle — distance is missing, so rearrange again: distance = speed × time. 1.0 cm/s × 30. s = 30. cm = 0.30 m.

Concept Two

Manipulating Equations

Every formula in this course can be solved for any of its variables — not just the one it's written for. Manipulating an equation means rearranging it algebraically until the variable you need is alone on one side.

✓

The 5-step method: (1) eliminate any fractions, (2) isolate the variable you want, (3) use simple algebra — the same operation on both sides, (4) show every step, (5) check your work and make sure no fraction is left in the denominator.

Worked example — solve p = mv for v

  1. Start with the original equation.p = mv
  2. "v" is multiplied by "m." Multiplication is undone by division — divide both sides by m.p/m = mv/m
  3. The "m" on the right cancels, leaving v alone.v = p/m

Worked example — solve a = (vf − vo)/t for t

This one has a fraction, so step 1 is to clear it first.

  1. Multiply both sides by t to clear the fraction.a·t = vf − vo
  2. Now t is multiplied by a — divide both sides by a to isolate t.(a·t)/a = (vf − vo)/a
  3. The "a" on the left cancels, leaving t alone.t = (vf − vo)/a
⚠

Common slip: after clearing a fraction, students often forget to divide every term on the other side. Whatever you do to one side of the equation, do to the entire other side — not just one term of it.

Practice Set 1

Rearrange It

Solve each equation for the requested variable. Show every step before you check your answer.

Problem 1force & mass

Newton's second law is F = ma. Solve for m.

Check your answer

Divide both sides by a: F/a = ma/a → the a's cancel on the right.

m = F/a

Problem 2distance & time

The constant-velocity equation is d = vt. Solve for t.

Check your answer

Divide both sides by v: d/v = vt/v → the v's cancel on the right.

t = d/v

Problem 3acceleration

Starting from a = (vf − vo)/t, solve for vf.

Check your answer

Clear the fraction: at = vf − vo. Then isolate vf by adding vo to both sides.

vf = at + vo

Problem 4acceleration

From the same equation, solve for vo instead.

Check your answer

Clear the fraction: at = vf − vo. This time isolate vo by adding it to both sides, then subtracting at from both sides.

vo = vf − at

Problem 5density

Density is D = m/V. Solve for V — remember, don't leave a fraction in the denominator.

Check your answer

Multiply both sides by V to clear the fraction: DV = m. Then divide both sides by D.

V = m/D

Practice Set 2

Calculate It

Use a = (vf − vo)/t for each real-world scenario. Watch your sign — a negative answer means the object is slowing down.

Problem 1track & field

A sprinter starts from rest and reaches 10.0 m/s in 2.5 s. What is her acceleration?

Check your answer

a = (vf − vo)/t = (10.0 − 0)/2.5

a = 4.0 m/s²

Problem 2driving

A car cruising at 28 m/s brakes to a complete stop in 7.0 s. Find its acceleration.

Check your answer

a = (vf − vo)/t = (0 − 28)/7.0

a = −4.0 m/s² (negative: the car is decelerating)

Problem 3elevators

An elevator starts at rest and reaches 3.0 m/s upward in 1.5 s. Find its acceleration.

Check your answer

a = (vf − vo)/t = (3.0 − 0)/1.5

a = 2.0 m/s²

Problem 4amusement parks

A roller coaster car crests a hill at 5.0 m/s, then speeds down the drop, reaching 25 m/s after 4.0 s. Find its acceleration.

Check your answer

a = (vf − vo)/t = (25 − 5.0)/4.0

a = 5.0 m/s²

Problem 5conceptual

Skater A speeds up from 0 to 5.0 m/s in 2.0 s. Skater B slows from 5.0 m/s to 0 in 5.0 s. Which skater has the greater magnitude of acceleration?

Check your answer

Skater A: a = 5.0/2.0 = 2.5 m/s². Skater B: a = −5.0/5.0 = −1.0 m/s², magnitude 1.0 m/s².

Skater A accelerates more — the same speed change happened in less time.

Putting It Together

One Problem, Both Skills

Applicationhighway merge

A car merges onto the highway at 8.0 m/s and accelerates at a steady 3.0 m/s². Using the manipulated form of the acceleration equation from Problem 3 above, what is the car's velocity after 4.0 s?

Check your answer

Rearranged form: vf = at + vo

vf = (3.0)(4.0) + 8.0 = 12 + 8.0

vf = 20 m/s

Notice the two skills chained together: rearranging the equation first made the calculation a single plug-and-chug step.
